IdeaCollage: Leveraging Generative AI for Design Ideation using Multi-Depth Collage

Weichen Sun, Buwei Zhou, Xuanzhu Zhao, Lu Ying, Junxiu Tang, Tan Tang

AI-assisted design exploration and multi-depth collage.

Read original paper (PDF) ↗Demo video ↓
DEMONSTRATION

System video

01

Introduction

Generative AI can produce many visual ideas quickly, but convincing images can also make designers settle on a direction too early. IdeaCollage connects AI-assisted exploration with collage so designers can revisit, combine and reorganize ideas instead of treating each generated image as a finished proposal.

Designing through exploration and refinement

The problem is not simply a shortage of generated images. Ideation moves repeatedly between expanding a design space and selecting, interpreting and refining possibilities. A linear conversation can make earlier text and image alternatives harder to recover; a polished image can also appear more resolved than the underlying idea. The research asks how an interface can retain these alternatives as working materials, so a designer can construct a new direction by recombining parts rather than repeatedly requesting a finished image.

02

Formative Study

Six experienced designers used generative AI and collage for design ideation. Two recurring stages emerged: keyword and image expansion, followed by image composition. The principal challenges were constrained exploration and poor navigability, and difficulty organizing growing collections of visual fragments.

From observations to design requirements

The formative study examined how designers move from keyword expansion and visual generation to composition. The identified difficulties include maintaining broad exploration, tracing the semantic context associated with images, preparing fragments and organizing the relationships between them. The system therefore connects navigable text–image exploration with editable visual material and a spatial organization mechanism.

03

Method / IdeaCollage

The interface has two coordinated workspaces. Dual-Chat separates text and image generation but keeps their mappings visible; the Multi-Depth Collage View lets designers crop, arrange and navigate visual fragments in depth.

How the two workspaces cooperate

The dual-chat view retains keyword branches and their associated image responses, allowing a designer to return to an earlier direction or develop another branch without losing its context. Images can then become editable fragments rather than fixed outputs. In the collage workspace, front and depth views serve different purposes: the former supports planar composition while the latter makes layering and relative depth available for inspection and rearrangement. Automatic layout is an initialization aid; semantic feature extraction, clustering and image ranking provide an initial arrangement that the designer can revise. Depth functions as an organizational structure rather than just an aesthetic effect.

  1. Dual-Chat ViewExplore and revisit keyword branches, then generate or revise images linked to those branches.
  2. Multi-Depth CollageExtract fragments, adjust their placement and depth, and inspect relationships from front and depth views.
  3. Auto-LayoutExtract CLIP features, cluster similar images with DBSCAN, rank images using entropy and maximum prototype similarity, then initialize overlap, position, scale and depth.

01 / Dual-chat: conceptual structure and cross-modal retrieval

The language model decomposes an initial brief into a three-level hierarchy of core concepts, sub-directions and detailed descriptors. Extend generates new candidates under a node, Modify changes its semantic focus, Delete removes an irrelevant branch, and Generate sends selected descriptors to image generation. Generated or edited images are transcribed into descriptors; CLIP similarity to the existing descriptor matrix locates the nearest concept. Bidirectional selection then allows the designer to recover either the text associated with an image or the visuals associated with a keyword.

02 / Fragment preparation and editable layout

Segment Anything proposes masks in the generated image. The designer selects useful objects, which become individually manipulable assets. The layout initializer computes normalized CLIP embeddings, groups them with DBSCAN, forms semantic prototypes and ranks fragments using the entropy and maximum similarity heuristic described in Algorithm 1. The resulting arrangement is only an initial suggestion: all placement, scale and depth decisions remain editable.

03 / Depth as an organizational medium

A fragment stores planar position, depth, scale, bounding box and rotation. Relative perceptual depth depends on both the layer and camera positions; perspective division changes apparent scale and displacement as the camera moves. Layers behind the camera are hidden. A link-map attribute connects a designated fragment to a target canvas and camera state, so traversal can pass between related sub-collages. These operations allow foreground/background, sequence and supporting relationships to be expressed without forcing a fixed taxonomy.

04

Evaluation / User study

Twelve additional designers completed a counterbalanced within-subjects study with IdeaCollage, AI-only and Depth-only configurations. The evaluation examined creativity-support items, usability, convergence and task duration.

Interpreting the comparison

The three conditions isolate the contribution of structured AI exploration and multi-depth composition. Relative to AI-only, the supplied draft reports higher Results Worth Effort, Enjoyment, Expressiveness, Immersion and Convergence ratings. Relative to Depth-only, it reports higher Exploration but lower Immersion; several other items did not differ significantly. Task duration also reveals a trade-off: the complete system took more time than AI-only and less than Depth-only. All twelve designers described depth as helping them make sense of fragmented visual material, while nine described using it to distinguish focal from supporting elements. These are findings of the supplied draft, not evidence that every dimension improves simultaneously. The original statistical table remains linked below.

In the supplied draft, IdeaCollage received higher ratings than AI-only for expressiveness, enjoyment, immersion and convergence, while taking longer. Compared with Depth-only, exploration and functional integration were rated higher and task time was shorter. These comparisons describe the supplied manuscript version; consult its statistical table for exact values.

ARCHITECTURAL IDEATION · RESEARCH MANUSCRIPT

IdeationArch: Mitigating Design Fixation in GenAI-supported Architectural Ideation

Exploring architectural alternatives across text, images and 3D.

Read original paper (PDF) ↗Demo video ↓
DEMONSTRATION

System video

Open demo video in a new tab ↗

01

Introduction

Architectural ideation involves text, images and preliminary 3D models. In linear AI workflows, earlier alternatives become difficult to revisit, and facade images can dominate later decisions before interior spaces are explored.

Why architecture is a special case

Architectural concepts must reconcile exterior form with internal organization. Text-to-image and image-to-3D transitions can amplify the influence of a selected facade: an image may become the conditioning reference for later generations, independently generated interior views may fail to correspond to it, and facade-conditioned 3D output may omit the internal spatial structure. The work studies these observable fixation-related behaviors across the whole ideation workflow, rather than treating each generation step in isolation.

02

Formative Study

Six professional architectural designers used GenAI for architectural ideation. Two challenges emerged: comparing multiple directions in a linear conversation, and maintaining attention to interiors when facade representations become the main reference. These findings informed five design requirements.

From conversation to spatial exploration

The six-designer inquiry motivates two complementary interaction requirements: maintain alternatives for parallel comparison instead of burying them in a linear transcript, and preserve interior design as an explicit object of exploration even after facade imagery has been generated. This motivates a representation in which semantic alternatives, visual candidates and interior examinations remain connected.

03

Method / IdeationArch

The system connects textual exploration with a staged sketch, white-model and 3D workflow. Designers compare alternatives using a multi-coxcomb concentric map, select nodes into a design block, and use cutaway editing to explore possible interiors.

Design state and cross-modal iteration

The concentric map externalizes the hierarchy of alternatives so the designer can compare related and distinct concepts at a glance. Selected concepts are brought into a design block and progressively represented in sketches, white models and preliminary 3D. The cutaway interaction makes a selected interior region accessible from a facade image; resulting visual changes are associated with versions of the design state rather than silently replacing the underlying intent. The MMR selection mechanism supports diversity among candidate concepts while retaining relevance. Generated interior views and meshes are exploratory representations, not verified construction models.

  1. Maximum Marginal Relevance (MMR)Generate at least ten candidates, use CLIP text embeddings to score relevance and redundancy, and retain five. The manuscript sets λ = 0.3.
  2. Reactive keyword dataflowRegenerate unlocked descendants when their parent changes. Locked branches remain unchanged.
  3. Cutaway-inspired visual explorationUse an accepted design state to generate axonometric sketches and white-model images. Mask a region to explore an interior, then generate a preliminary 3D mesh on demand.

01 / Generating and organizing alternatives

The multi-coxcomb map distinguishes core design directions from interior and facade content. At least ten candidate keywords are generated per request in the supplied manuscript. MMR combines CLIP-based relevance and redundancy scores, selects five candidates iteratively, and incorporates previously selected items in its reference set. Designers can extend, modify, reference and lock nodes; these operations make exploration and controlled propagation explicit.

02 / Staged visual generation and design state

A design block collects selected nodes from different branches into a versioned state. Instead of directly requesting a finished 3D building, the workflow generates axonometric sketches, then white-model imagery and finally a mesh conditioned on accepted visual material. A designer can stop at any stage, compare alternatives or modify the underlying semantic content.

03 / Interior cutaway and version management

A mask delineates the region of an accepted axonometric image that should reveal interior space. The system generates a cutaway guided by the selected interior content and uses the existing image as context. Direct visual edits and semantic edits are stored as distinct revisions. Earlier accepted versions remain available; a new design block starts a new exploration rather than inheriting an unwanted image.

The system separates semantic design content from accepted visual results using a versioned design state.

04

Evaluation

Twelve architectural designers compared IdeationArch with a baseline in a within-subjects study. Variety and originality were higher (r = 0.843 and 0.861, both p < .01); fluency did not differ significantly. Participants also performed more interior-related activity, and rated the system higher on the measured CSI dimensions.

What the evidence establishes

The comparison distinguishes fluency (number of ideas), variety (distinct categories) and originality (less common categories). The supplied manuscript reports greater variety and originality without a significant fluency increase. Interaction counts also show more interior-related activity during both textual and visual exploration; the visual-stage difference between facade and interior interactions is smaller with IdeationArch. These counts describe interaction behavior rather than time allocation. Interview responses help explain how parallel comparison and spatial inspection support perceived control; they do not establish that fixation disappears in every setting.

Organizing Many-Agent Reasoning for Rare-Disease Diagnosis

Weichen Sun

Organizing specialist LLM agents for rare-disease reasoning.

Read original paper (PDF) ↗Code ↗
01

Introduction

Rare-disease cases can require knowledge across specialties. Selecting a small team too early may miss useful expertise; involving every agent in every discussion increases communication cost. HCR separates broad screening from detailed discussion.

Scaling participation, not just agent count

Rare-disease diagnosis requires hypotheses from incomplete evidence and decisions about what further information to acquire. Expanding a flat discussion can add expertise, but also raises communication costs and may introduce redundant or competing suggestions. HCR separates the availability of specialty knowledge from the number of agents performing detailed deliberation on an individual case.

02

Method / Many-Agent Organization

Hierarchical Committee Routing (HCR) organizes agents into clinical departments. Each chair independently screens the case; only the selected committees instantiate specialist agents for a local pass.

Departmental routing and adjudication

The chairs independently produce structured screening briefs, including relevance and missing evidence. The router chooses a bounded local participation budget: two committees at mean relevance of at least 75, otherwise three. A deterministic keyword-based fallback fills invalid or incomplete selections. Selected departments deliberate locally, then produce structured summaries. The Board aggregates these records and the final judge receives both the individual summaries and the aggregation alongside the case. This design preserves broad initial coverage without requiring every department to participate in a shared full-length discussion.

  1. Broad screeningEach chair provides relevance, evidence, missing information, uncertainty and candidate diagnoses.
  2. Selective local participationActivate two committees when mean relevance is at least 75; otherwise activate three. Invalid or incomplete router selections are filled using a deterministic keyword score.
  3. Structured upward communicationSummarizers produce bounded committee records. The Board merges fields, removes exact duplicates and averages confidence.
  4. Final adjudicationThe judge receives the original case, individual committee records and Board aggregation; it returns a primary diagnosis, differentials and recommended tests.

01 / Independent screening and routing

Each available chair reads the original clinical case independently and returns relevance, evidence, uncertainty and candidate diagnostic directions. Mean relevance of at least 75 activates two committees; lower relevance activates three. An LLM router proposes departments, then a deterministic fallback combines configured department priority and case-insensitive keyword matches to fill invalid or missing choices.

02 / Selective deliberation and structured communication

Only activated committees instantiate local members. Members contribute once in a fixed order and can inspect earlier contributions within their committee. A summarizer receives the original case and up to 600 characters from each contribution, and emits bounded hypotheses, evidence, missing information, tests and minority opinions. The communication boundary controls context growth without broadcasting all raw deliberations.

03 / Board integration and final adjudication

The Board aggregates structured records deterministically, normalizing and deduplicating exact strings while retaining committee provenance. It does not invent additional diagnoses. The final judge receives the case, the individual committee records and Board aggregation, returning one primary diagnosis, up to six differential diagnoses and examination recommendations. The architecture is evaluated as a research benchmark, not a clinical deployment.

03

Evaluation / Experiments and Analysis

The evaluation uses 302 rare-disease cases across 33 categories, comparing HCR against MAC on Qwen, DeepSeek and Gemini. HCR improves primary-consultation most-likely and possible-diagnosis accuracy by 6.96–9.61 and 4.30–7.28 percentage points, respectively. Gemini most-likely accuracy rises from 54.97% to 63.91% as available departments increase from four to sixteen, while test helpfulness remains below MAC. These are benchmark results, not clinical validation.

Diagnostic accuracy versus examination planning

The benchmark separates the primary diagnosis from possible diagnoses and the helpfulness of recommended examinations. The scaling experiment keeps Gemini fixed while expanding the available department pool; the most-likely diagnosis score rises from 54.97% with four departments to 63.91% with sixteen. However, the reported test-helpfulness score declines as the pool grows and remains lower than MAC across all three backbones. The result is a specific accuracy–examination-planning trade-off, rather than a claim that more agents improve all clinical decisions. The study evaluates benchmark reasoning and does not establish clinical safety or deployment readiness.

JOURNAL OF ZHEJIANG UNIVERSITY (SCIENCE EDITION) · ONLINE FIRST · 2026

TimeFolder: An Adaptive Compression Framework for Time-Oriented Data Visualization

Adaptive compression of time series for visualization.

唐谈，孙炜辰，巩悦，刘书含，刘洋，巫英才

浙江大学学报（理学版） · 网络首发 2026-09-01
DOI: 10.3785/1008-9497.26183

Read published paper (PDF) ↗Online demo ↗DOI ↗
01

Introduction

Long time series can create crowded charts in which important patterns become hard to see. TimeFolder compresses the timeline according to the information that matters for the visualization, rather than applying a uniform compression rule.

Preserve the visual task, not every sample

A uniform temporal aggregation can erase a short but meaningful event while spending the same visual space on long stable periods. TimeFolder instead treats compression as a visualization problem: retain the temporal distinctions needed for a target analysis and fold redundant neighboring frames. Figure 1 compares the original airport relationship visualization with TimeFolder, PAA and RNNA and illustrates how adjacent frames can be represented by a folded timeline.

02

Method / Problem Definition and Framework

A folded timeline aggregates adjacent time frames. A Minimum Description Length (MDL)-inspired objective balances model complexity against the cost of preserving visual information. Four Visual Information (VI) functions target trend changes, relative balance across series, categorical duration and switching, and temporal relationship strength.

The objective and visual-information functions

The representation balances the complexity of the folded timeline against the information lost by representing the original data through it. The MDL-inspired objective provides a common structure, while the VI function determines which visual pattern matters. Trend changes, cross-series balance, categorical durations and transitions, and temporal relationships need not be valued in the same way; specifying the intended visual task makes the compression adaptive rather than uniformly resampling the series.

01 / Define task-aware visual information

The original timeline is represented as an ordered set of frames. Folding combines adjacent frames; the cost of a candidate partition depends on how much visual information the compressed chart loses and the complexity of the representation. For a temperature chart, the information function emphasizes changes and joint trends; for a relationship chart, it emphasizes connection patterns. The function must be designed for the analytical task rather than assumed universal.

03

Method / TimeFolder Algorithm and Analysis

The algorithm searches for an adaptive time partition using randomized restarts, hierarchical clustering and FindMDL tree cutting.

Search procedure

The algorithm explores alternative initial folds and builds candidate hierarchical trees through adjacent temporal clustering. FindMDL then compares the description-length cost of retaining a parent interval with the cost of representing its children separately, selecting a lower-cost partition within each candidate tree. Randomized restarts explore alternative candidates; this is a search procedure, not a guarantee of global optimality for arbitrary inputs.

  1. Randomized restartGenerate alternative folding initializations to explore multiple candidate trees.
  2. Hierarchical clusteringMerge adjacent time frames according to their description-length-based similarity.
  3. FindMDLTraverse candidate trees and compare the cost of a parent node with the cost of keeping its children. Choose the lower-cost cut.

02 / Generate and score candidate timeline partitions

Adjacent-frame clustering builds a hierarchical tree of possible temporal groups. Randomized starts produce alternative trees, and the MDL-inspired objective scores cuts of each tree to find a compact candidate with low information loss. The repetition count controls search effort. The final result is a nonuniform timeline whose folded intervals can be rendered with their temporal context intact.

04

Application Cases

The two cases are daily temperature trends (line chart) and airport departure schedules (relationship chart). The different VI functions allow the same framework to prioritize different visual patterns.

Two different visual tasks

The daily temperature example uses a line-chart task to preserve important changes, such as abrupt cooling and subsequent recovery, without retaining all redundant samples. The airport scheduling example uses a circular relationship representation where temporal connections between nodes matter. A fold can therefore preserve different information in the two cases even though the overarching optimization framework is the same.

05

Evaluation / Expert and performance study

The manuscript compares TimeFolder with PAA and RNNA through expert ratings, trend preservation and runtime. In the line-chart case, runtimes are 0.20 s, 0.33 s and 1.85 s respectively; in the relationship-chart case, 0.048 s, 0.87 s and 1.38 s. Results are specific to these cases and configurations.

Evidence and scope

The evaluation compares visual quality, retention of important patterns and computation time against PAA and RNNA. The expert assessment uses four experts; its mean ratings should be interpreted descriptively rather than as a large-sample statistical inference. In addition to the reported runtimes, the original tables provide parameters and other performance measures for the two tasks. The observed advantage concerns the supplied datasets and task definitions; it is not a universal speed or compression guarantee.

ART THERAPY · LEONARDO MANUSCRIPT

Expressing the Inexpressible: The Semantics of Depth in Therapeutic Collage

Weichen Sun, Tan Tang

Using semantic depth to support expressive therapeutic collage.

Read original paper (PDF) ↗
01

Introduction / Expressing the Inexpressible

Patients with emotional communication impairments may have rich experiences that are difficult to express verbally. This work studies whether depth in digital collage can help communicate relationships, memories and emotional distance, rather than merely creating a visual effect.

Expression before verbal explanation

For people with emotional communication impairments, difficulty in verbal articulation does not imply an absence of emotional or autobiographical experience. The study investigates a visual environment in which personally meaningful images can be introduced and organized as material for subsequent therapeutic dialogue, rather than requiring a fully formed verbal narrative at the outset.

02

Collage, Aphasia, and Depth

For people with aphasia, image fragments can make experiences available for discussion before a complete verbal narrative is possible. Layered depth provides a spatial way to express nearness, distance, emphasis and transitions.

Depth as a semantic resource

A foreground element may represent immediate pressure or importance, while a background element may mark a memory, distance or contextual relationship. These meanings are not fixed labels imposed by the system; they emerge from the patient’s arrangement and supported discussion with the therapist. The objective is to offer additional expressive structure without replacing collaborative interpretation.

03

Practice-Based Inquiry

Interviews with two experienced art therapists identified three practical needs: more patient-driven image sourcing, integrated image preparation, and clearer organization of relationships between visual elements.

Requirements from therapeutic practice

Interviews with two therapists informed the design goals. Image sourcing should allow patients to introduce personally meaningful material; preparation tools should reduce interruptions during composition; and spatial relationships should remain available for expression and later interpretation. These requirements connect the technical workflow to the actual therapeutic making and discussion process.

04

Method / Multi-Depth Collage

MDC provides a workspace with three design goals: patient-driven sourcing, integrated preparation, and semantic depth.

From image sourcing to supported dialogue

MDC combines patient-controlled image upload and generation with in-workspace editing. Patients can crop, mask and arrange fragments, adjust their depth and navigate the composition through a camera-like view. Linked canvases allow separate episodes to be connected. The therapist first introduces the prompt in accessible language, then uses the completed work as a shared reference for discussion. The system is a communication aid, not an autonomous therapist.

  1. Patient-driven sourcingUpload personally meaningful images or use text-to-image and image-to-image generation.
  2. Integrated preparationCrop, resize, mask, segment and adjust opacity inside the collage workspace.
  3. Semantic depth and traversalArrange images at different depths and move through the collage using a camera-like viewport. Use linked canvases to organize episodes.

01 / Source and prepare material with patient control

Patients choose personally meaningful images from uploads or generated alternatives. Integrated cropping, masking, segmentation, resizing and opacity controls allow preparation without switching tools. The purpose is to preserve the patient’s own associations and expressive continuity; automation proposes materials but does not determine their symbolic meaning.

02 / Organize relationships through depth

Patients place fragments in the front view and adjust their depth in a separate depth view. Camera traversal makes proximity, concealment, layering and transitions visible. Linked canvases can separate episodes or themes while maintaining paths between them. No fixed psychological meaning is assigned to a particular layer: a foreground item might represent pressure, importance or closeness depending on the patient’s interpretation.

03 / Use the composition in therapeutic dialogue

The therapist presents a prompt and helps the patient operate the workspace as needed, then explores the resulting composition together with them. Questions can focus on particular images, the relationships between them or why one element is closer than another. The collage provides a shared reference for narration and clarification; it is not an automatic treatment recommendation or diagnostic model.

05

Evaluation

Twelve patients with post-stroke aphasia and four art therapists evaluated MDC against conventional 2D collage in a within-subject comparison. Patient measures included expressive completeness, externalization, complex experience and agency. Therapist measures included narrative clarity and symbolic coherence.

Patient, therapist and behavioral evidence

The counterbalanced comparison uses the same therapeutic prompt in both conditions. Patient measures address expressive completeness, emotional externalization, complex experience and agency; therapist measures address narrative clarity, interpretation, symbolic coherence and usefulness for dialogue. Behavioral measures capture discussion duration, patient-initiated elaborations, clarification requests and topics elicited. The manuscript reports significant gains in all four patient measures, narrative clarity, symbolic coherence, elaborations and topics. Discussion duration and clarification requests did not reach significance. Interviews describe images as entry points into narration, uninterrupted preparation and depth as a means of organizing emotional relationships. The sample concerns post-stroke aphasia; the findings should not be generalized to all emotional communication impairments without further study.

Expressive completeness rose from 3.67 to 5.67 (p = .012); ease of externalization from 3.25 to 5.00 (p = .008). Patient-initiated elaborations increased from 3.08 to 4.08, and therapeutic topics from 2.83 to 4.75. Narrative clarity and symbolic coherence improved; ease of interpretation and usefulness for dialogue did not reach significance.
